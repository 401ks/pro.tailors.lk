eXPerience 5 vs Tailors.lk Pro — The Designer's Tool and the Shop's Engine

One product catalogues the design language of a tailoring business. The other runs the shop that turns designs into money. Both are real. They serve two different jobs — here is how to tell them apart.

Last verified: August 8, 2026 · Sources: public materials only, nothing estimated.

Every Tailoring Business Has Two Jobs

Two jobs run at once in every shop. One of them decides whether a business looks like that, and one decides whether it stays in business. Pick the wrong tool for the wrong job and the whole shop feels it.

The creative job

Designs, fabrics, work types — the vision that makes a customer choose you. This is the language your shop speaks to the world, and some tools are built entirely to organize that language.

The operational job

Measurements, orders, deadlines, advances, receipts — the machinery that turns the vision into money. When this job breaks, the business loses money in front of customers.

Some software serves one job. Some serves the other. Confusing the two is the most common mistake in this comparison.

The job 5 does

eXPerience 5 is documented as a "tailoring and fashion design software" that helps users "manage tailors, fabric types, design and work types, and client measurement profiles in an organized and efficient way."

Read that list again and notice its center of gravity: fabric types, design types, work types. Its gravity is creative cataloguing — organizing the design language of a tailoring business.

  • Design and work-type cataloguing — its documented core.
  • Client measurement profiles
  • Tailor and fabric management

Two honest limits on their side

Judged strictly on the public record, two things are unanswered:

  • Pricing is not public. Sources reference requesting a free demo, with no published figure.
  • Operational depth — production tracking, billing, customer self-service — is not detailed in the materials we verified.

The Shop's Engine

Our gravity is the other job — the shop's engine.

  • The production line, made visible. Every order travels a six-stage board: Inbound, Cutting, Stitching, QC, Ready, Completed. Deadlines carry automatic overdue alerts, so late deliveries get caught before the customer notices.
  • The measurement memory. A versioned vault with templates for shirts, suits, trousers, and traditional wear — every fit a customer has ever had, preserved and reusable.
  • The money trail. Branded, one-click PDF receipts with advance paid, balance due, and auto subtotals — in LKR.
  • The customer's window. Private tracking links let customers watch their own order's progress without calling the shop.
  • The shop's face. A public profile with your branding and cover photo.

The price of running that engine today: nothing. Free to start, no card, no countdown on core features.

Could you use both?

Potentially, yes — and we say that without irony. A design-forward studio might catalogue its creative world in a design-oriented tool while running production, money, and customer communication on Tailors.lk Pro. The two jobs can have two tools.

But if you are choosing one place to start — and most Sri Lankan shops are — start with the job that loses you money when it breaks: missed deadlines, lost measurements, unrecorded advances. That job is free to take on today.

The unanswered questions to ask them

If eXPerience 5 interests you, take these to the demo call: What does it cost? Does it track production stages and deadlines? Can customers check an order's status themselves? Does it bill in Rupees? The answers tell you whether you are buying a design companion or a shop engine.
