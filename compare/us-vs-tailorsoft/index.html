Where Your Software Comes From Matters

Software carries the fingerprints of the market that raised it — its tax rules, its payment habits, its languages, its idea of what a tailor shop looks like. This comparison is largely a story about geography.

TailorSoft's Passport

TailorSoft is documented as a cloud-based tailoring shop solution for "ladies and gents tailoring shops," built by a company whose profile identifies it as an enterprise software development company serving clients in the United States.

Its documented feature set centers on administration and control:

  • Secure login system with user-level restrictions
  • Customer measurement records and bookings
  • Management of purchases, sales, and purchase returns

Its documented pricing has two parts:

  • Registration fee: USD 120 — cited as PKR 33,360 in the source
  • Monthly subscription: USD 20 per branch — cited as PKR 5,560

Note the structure

An upfront registration fee, then a recurring per-branch charge, in US dollars. You pay to enter, and you pay for every branch after.

What this means in rupees

The source quotes the registration fee as PKR 33,360 and the monthly fee as PKR 5,560 per branch. Convert that to life at a Sri Lankan counter: to open the door is a USD-level commitment, and the recurring meter never stops — whether the shop across the street is your second branch or your first.

Reading the Map

Three geographic facts follow from that profile.

First, the currency

USD pricing means your software cost floats against the rupee. When the exchange rate moves, your overhead moves with it — in a business where margins are stitched one garment at a time.

Second, the per-branch model

$20/month per branch is a sensible model for chains, and a needless surcharge structure for the single shop that most Sri Lankan tailoring is. If you will never open branch two, you are paying for a scaling model you will not use — after paying $120 for the privilege of starting.

Third, the feature accent

User-level restrictions, purchase returns, bookings — the vocabulary of structured retail administration. Powerful where that is the shape of your business. Notably quieter on the things that dominate a Sri Lankan counter: LKR receipts with advances and balances, customers checking their own order status, and WhatsApp — the channel your customers already live on.

The View From Here

Tailors.lk Pro was built a few kilometers from your shop, not a few thousand. What that means in practice:

The money speaks LKR

Receipts show advance paid and balance due in rupees, auto-computed, branded with your logo and footer, generated in one click.

The workflow speaks to the tailor

Six stages — Inbound, Cutting, Stitching, QC, Ready, Completed — on a board you can drag with a thumb, with overdue alerts chasing the work.

The measurements remember

A versioned vault with templates for shirts, suits, trousers, and traditional wear, so every regular's fit is one tap away.

The customers help themselves

Each order carries a private tracking link. "Is it ready?" answers itself.

The door is free

No registration fee. No per-branch meter. No card on file.
