Buttonstripe vs Tailors.lk Pro — A Tale of Two Tailor Shops

Two software tools. Two tailor shops on the same busy Saturday. One was built for a tailor's shop in Lagos — the other for yours, right here in Sri Lanka.

Last verified: August 8, 2026

Picture Your Shop for a Moment

It is a busy Saturday. Three customers are waiting. One wants to know if his trousers are ready. Another is paying an advance. The third is new, and you need her measurements before you can start.

This is the moment software either earns its place or gets closed forever.

Now Picture Buttonstripe in That Moment

Buttonstripe calls itself "The Tailor's OS" — a business and inventory tool for tailors. In that Saturday rush, it would let you manage customer measurements, orders, invoices, and delivery updates in one place. It gives you a fashion storefront where customers can browse designs and place orders online, an integrated chat to keep every customer conversation organized, and invoicing with payment tracking.

It is a genuinely appealing package. The storefront angle is its signature move — turning a tailor into an online shop.

Then you reach the pricing page.

The free plan lasts 30 days. After that, the Pro plan costs NGN 15,000 per month. NGN — Nigerian Naira. Buttonstripe's language, imagery, and currency all point at Nigeria and the broader African market. For your shop in Galle or Kandy, that means three problems at once:

  1. 1
    A currency you do not earn in. Your revenue is LKR. Your software bill would be tied to Naira.
  2. 2
    A trial, not a free tool. Thirty days to decide whether your entire shop runs on this system. Then the meter starts.
  3. 3
    A market that is not yours. Features, payment flows, and support shaped around African tailoring commerce, not Sri Lankan.

The Same Saturday, Running on Tailors.lk Pro

Back to your shop. Same rush, different tools.

The new customer's measurements go into a vault that remembers every version of her fit — shirt, trouser, or traditional wear, each with its own template. The customer paying an advance gets a branded LKR receipt showing exactly what he paid and what remains, generated in one click with your shop's logo and footer. The customer asking about his trousers? He already has a tracking link. He checked it over breakfast. He knows his order moved from Stitching to QC this morning. He never called you at all.

And when the month ends, the bill for all of that is the same as the day you signed up: nothing. No 30-day countdown. No NGN conversion. No card on file.

Fair Words for Buttonstripe

The storefront vision is real, and for a Nigerian tailor building an online fashion brand, Buttonstripe is aimed squarely at them. The integrated chat and the marketplace thinking show genuine product ambition. If your shop lives in Lagos, this page would read very differently.

Where We Take You Next

The features you would miss most are already in motion. Automated WhatsApp order updates and payment reminders are in development — built for the way Sri Lankan tailors actually talk to customers. A richer public shop presence is part of the roadmap. When online payments arrive, they will run on the rails your customers already trust: PayHere, Genie, LankaQR.

But the Saturday rush does not wait for roadmaps. The board, the vault, the receipts, and the tracking links are live right now.
