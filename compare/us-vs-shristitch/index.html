ShriStitch vs Tailors.lk Pro — The Arithmetic of Choosing

Every good decision starts with a number. ShriStitch publishes one — ₹20,000 a year — and that number does real work once converted into a Sri Lankan tailor shop's budget ledger. This is that arithmetic, line by line.

Last verified: August 8, 2026 · Sources: public materials only, nothing estimated.

The published tiers

ShriStitch is an Indian tailoring and fabric management software covering "the overall process of tailoring & boutique working." Its pricing comes in two published tiers:

  • ₹20,000 / year — "All features, one price"
  • ₹35,000 / year — demo / request tier

What that means in LKR

The intelligence report translates the pricing to approximately LKR 80,000–140,000 per year for a Sri Lankan buyer.

Read that second number again. For many tailor shops, that is not a software budget. That is a sewing machine. That is fabric stock. That is a month of rent.

First line in the owner's ledger

Before the first login, before a single measurement is saved, the annual LKR 80,000 is already spent. Whatever the software later earns must first pay that figure back.

What ₹20,000 a Year Buys

To be precise about what is on offer, ShriStitch's documented feature set is ERP-grade:

  • Worker Wise Wage Rate
  • Work Allocation and Collection
  • Wage Rate System
  • Process Management
  • Payroll and Wage
  • Email / SMS capabilities
  • Multi-language support
  • Barcode stock tracking

Its own site positions it as "exclusively designed for boutiques, fashion, and tailoring stores," and the company's story leans on its roots in India's tailoring tradition. Against that price, none of it is in dispute — the question is only whether it serves a working Sri Lankan shop.

The Cost-Benefit Ledger

Five questions any shopowner would ask, answered honestly.

Question The honest answer
Is the feature set serious?Yes. Payroll, wage rates, and work allocation are real operational tools.
Is it affordable for an average Sri Lankan shop?Approximately LKR 80,000/year is out of reach for most small shops.
Does it speak your customer's payment language?No documented PayHere, Genie, or Koko integration.
Is there a free way to test the depth?The published path is annual purchase or demo request.
Who is the natural buyer?A larger Indian boutique or workshop running staff payroll and piece-rate wages.

The Break-Even Reality

Ask it as a business question: how many extra orders must a shop complete — and actually get paid for — to justify LKR 80,000 before the software has earned its keep?

For a shop stitching at modest margins, the answer is measured in months of profit, spent before the first login.

The Other Column: LKR 0

Tailors.lk Pro enters the ledger differently. The entry cost is zero, and here is exactly what that zero covers today:

  • A six-stage order board — Inbound through Completed — with automatic overdue alerts, so deadlines stop slipping through
  • A measurement vault with version history and garment-specific templates, so no customer ever gets re-measured from scratch
  • Branded one-click PDF receipts in LKR with advance paid, balance due, and auto-calculated subtotals
  • Payment status at a glance: Unpaid, Partial, Fully Paid
  • Private tracking links, so customers answer their own "is it ready?" questions
  • A public shop profile with your branding and cover photo
  • Passwordless login and a mobile-first build, because the counter computer is usually a phone

The ROI calculation inverts. Instead of "how many orders to pay for the software," the question becomes "how many overdue orders, lost measurements, and unrecorded advances will this stop leaking?" Every rupee recovered is pure gain against a cost of zero.

The Bottom Line

If you run a large workshop with wage workers and you are buying in India, ShriShitch's ERP depth may justify its price. If you run a Sri Lankan tailor shop and the question is "how do I stop losing orders, measurements, and money without spending LKR 80,000 first," the arithmetic favors starting free.
